    01 / 05 · Grow

    A climber that needs support.

    Black pepper is the fruit of Piper nigrum, a perennial vine native to the Western Ghats. It climbs a living support tree by its aerial roots and bears its first crop in the third year.

    Botanical name
    Piper nigrum L.
    Support
    Living shade tree
    First harvest
    Year 3 · productive 20+ years

    Scene: pepper vines spiralling up tall support trees, leaves unfolding as they climb.

    A clump of leafy shoots.

    Green cardamom is the fruit of Elettaria cardamomum, a forest plant of the ginger family. From an underground rhizome it sends up tall shoots with long, glossy leaves, under 40–60 % shade.

    Botanical name
    Elettaria cardamomum
    Shade
    40–60 % filtered light
    First harvest
    Year 2–3 · productive 10–15 years

    Scene: cardamom clumps of tall leafy shoots growing under forest trees.

    02 / 05 · Fruit

    Green spikes, ripening slowly.

    Flowers form on hanging spikes after the monsoon. Over six to eight months each spike fills with tightly packed berries that turn from green to red.

    Spike length
    7–15 cm
    Development
    6–8 months
    Ripening
    Green → yellow → red

    Scene: hanging spikes of berries on the vine, ripening from green to red.

    Flowers at ground level.

    Cardamom flowers on long panicles that grow from the base of the plant and creep across the forest floor. Small white flowers give way to plump green capsules.

    Panicles
    From the base, along the ground
    Flower
    White, violet-streaked lip
    Maturity
    ≈ 120 days after flowering

    Scene: flower stalks spreading over the ground from the base of the plant, setting green capsules.

    03 / 05 · Harvest

    Picked by hand, spike by spike.

    Harvest starts when one or two berries on a spike turn red. Whole spikes are picked into baskets and the berries are then threshed from their stalks.

    Signal
    1–2 red berries per spike
    Method
    Hand-picked
    Threshing
    Manual or mechanical

    Scene: ripe spikes lifting from the vine into a woven basket.

    Picked green, pod by pod.

    Capsules are picked by hand when fully mature but still green, in rounds through the season. Picking at the right moment keeps both the colour and the aromatic oil.

    Picking rounds
    Every 15–30 days
    Method
    Hand-picked
    Stage
    Mature, still green

    Scene: green capsules lifting from the ground panicles into a woven basket.

    04 / 05 · Sun drying

    A week in the sun.

    Berries are spread thin on clean mats and turned through the day. As moisture leaves, the skin darkens and wrinkles into the black peppercorn.

    Drying time
    7–10 days
    Target moisture
    ≤ 12 %
    Dry recovery
    ≈ 1/3 of fresh weight

    Scene: a heap of berries raked flat on a woven mat, turning black in the sun.

    04 / 05 · Curing

    Cured to keep the green.

    Fresh capsules are cured on trays in a heated curing house rather than in the sun. Gentle, even heat draws out moisture while holding the deep green colour.

    Curing temp.
    45–50 °C
    Curing time
    ≈ 18–36 hours
    Target moisture
    ≤ 10 %

    Scene: green capsules spread on a wire-mesh curing tray, drying to olive green.
